## Study A: bounded physical settlement before semantic-only offscreen time

The new research-only settlement policy uses the existing PRSELoop and native GameCore/Rapier, not a second solver. It steps the 3D rigid bodies until all local dynamics have sustained low linear/angular speed, little displacement/rotation over twenty consecutive ticks and real collider contact evidence. Only then does it unload bodies, advance the remaining world clock and semantic processes, save an actually unloaded state, and rematerialize the same source IDs. If no safe settlement is established under the bounded budget, the policy refuses the optimization and continues real physics.

The inputs were the previous fixed 63 source-derived SceneEval descriptions containing an actual movable object: 28 original human-authored descriptions and 35 from the systematic partly assisted sample. Two fixed investigator-specified physical impulse strengths per scene produced 126 distinct physical episodes. Each episode started from identical GameCore snapshots. One path stepped all 300 physics ticks (five seconds). The other used the settlement policy, and actual physical positions, event sequences, source-typed factual status, source prose, ID persistence and save/re-entry were compared.

Results: 52/126 could unload under these criteria; 74 refused and kept full physics. The median offloaded episode skipped about 220 of 300 physical ticks. All 126 retained the tested authoritative global clock, source prose/IDs, physical event sequence and source-relation truth. Among the 52 offloaded episodes the worst final 3D position error against uninterrupted physics after a real resume tick was under 0.08 millimetres. Every accepted unloaded state was genuinely serialized offscreen and rematerialized. These are ticks skipped, not a verified application wall-time or render-GPU speedup.

Negative evidence: some episodes that never unloaded nevertheless showed centimetre-scale trajectory differences between independently reconstructed physics worlds. A two-millimetre error was reproduced in a full-physics control with alternate stepping. An earlier 1.4-centimetre error was not reproduced in three further full-physics controls, so its precise cause is unresolved. No hidden human playtest or general deterministic guarantee is asserted.

## Study B: known future physical actions expose an unsafe shortcut

Real PRSE offscreen events may require a later physical interaction. An initial two-event schedule policy could unload an apparently settled region before its next known action, rematerialize, apply an actual native GameCore impulse at the correct time and continue. In 28 human-authored scenes, it unloaded at least once in seventeen.

One externally authored scene, ID 27 with a movable cup, ended approximately 0.62 metres away from uninterrupted physics. Both cause-event sequence and the tested source-derived factual predicates still agreed, showing why event log parity is NOT sufficient physical consistency evidence.

Four matched paths exposed the mechanism: uninterrupted GameCore; always-physical but ordinary PRSE JSON save/reload at event boundaries; early offscreen unloading with no extra JSON save; and early unloading with JSON reloading. At tick 150 all cup poses agreed. After the next real impulse, ordinary JSON reconstruction AND early rematerialization differed from the uninterrupted reference by about 0.628 metres at tick 260 and 0.620 metres at tick 360. Saving only source identity, position, velocity and rotation does not preserve all contact-solver state needed for later contact-rich dynamics. The original failed raw v1 result and the independently reproduced four-path comparison are preserved.

## Study C: conservative future-action policy without contact reconstruction

A second policy keeps the same living GameCore/Rapier contact island resident through ALL explicitly known upcoming physical actions, applies those actions at their scheduled world timestamps, and may unload only after the final scheduled cause and verified settling.

Applied to the same 63 frozen source scenes with two known future impulses: 63/63 ran successfully; 23 could unload after the final event, skipping a combined 728 physics ticks. The other forty ran continuous physics. All intended action timestamps, measured physical event sequences, original literary source texts, persistent IDs, source-typed truth states and authoritative world times matched their uninterrupted comparisons. None had more than two centimetres final position error. Among the 23 actually unloaded after last action, the worst was below 0.08 millimetres. Across all cases the worst was approximately 2.75 millimetres in a case that did not unload. The source-27 62-centimetre failure disappeared when its contact state was kept resident.

This safety policy sacrifices some optimization. Its applicability depends on knowing the upcoming actions. Unexpected NPC interactions, incoming collisions from another region, heat/fire causing motion, and arbitrary new input may invalidate the assumption. Full-game character needs are not equivalent during semantic-only time; player hydration discrepancies were retained as negative evidence.

## Study D: native Rapier binary snapshot compared with PRSE JSON reconstruction

Rapier officially supports a complete native World.takeSnapshot and World.restoreSnapshot byte array. See https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/serialization/ . It also supports sleeping for inactive rigid bodies while they remain in the physics world; see https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies/ . Native snapshots are tied to the exact engine build and trusted inputs, not a stable user-facing interchange format.

Ran a matched solver-level serialization comparison on all 63 frozen source scenes with the same two future actual physical impulses. The native binary path had no final position difference above two centimetres and maximum source-body deviation about 0.185 millimetres. The JSON path had the one original source-27 cup failure around 56.6 centimetres. Median binary solver snapshot was approximately 62.5 KB versus 21.7 KB for the structured JSON. Native binary replica worlds still occasionally had small differences, so raw binary physics restoration alone is not proved universally bitwise deterministic.

## Study E: whole existing GameCore physical world plus its semantic record

A new isolated research-only native/semantic transaction captures the same structured PRSE and GameCore literary source, source-typed entities, semantic processes, world time and event records PLUS the complete native Rapier world. It stores exact handle mappings for existing GameCore player, crate, fence, stone, articulated ragdoll (ten bodies and nine joints), WorldBridge active semantic bodies, local source entities, floor, and research event-contact cache.

On restore, it verifies the same vendored engine build digest, complete binary checksum, semantic/handle integrity, source entity IDs and GameCore time; restores native Rapier; and rebinds those SAME existing GameCore components to their native restored handles. It does not introduce another physics authority or touch the production checkout. A separate integrity test rejected nine corrupted source/build/handle/time/binary variants and a nonexistent player body handle even when its simple file digest was recomputed. It is trusted-owner-input integrity, not cryptographic adversarial-file authentication.

Ran 63 externally source-derived full-GameCore continuations with two later physical impulses: uninterrupted reference, native/semantic transaction restore, and existing JSON reconstruction. All paths called actual GameCore.step, exercising source bodies, real joints/ragdoll, player, WorldBridge, world clock and event stream.

Results: 63/63 native transaction restores completed with ZERO measured final position difference for the tested dynamic entities, and no tested semantic truth, event sequence, global clock, player-position or ragdoll-torso discrepancies. Source prose/identities persisted in all 63. Existing JSON reconstruction again had one 56.6-cm failure. Median total JSON-packaged native/semantic transaction was about 107 KB versus 22 KB for current JSON, around five times as large.

Important limitation: native binary snapshots after continuation were NOT byte-identical in all scenes, and most complete GameCore JSON snapshots also differed. A direct control proved even taking a Rapier native snapshot, restoring it and immediately reserializing can change binary bytes although body/joint counts and measured states match. Therefore byte-hash inequality alone does not establish perceptible physical divergence, while matched tested poses/events do NOT establish universal full-world determinism. Other internal state, observer subscriptions, future NPC intentions and multi-region dependencies still require validation.

Native binary restoration currently snapshots the ENTIRE GameCore physical world. You cannot restore it later after unrelated regions have legitimately progressed without potentially rolling those regions back. Production must define region-level native physics ownership/islands and contact boundaries, atomic source/physics/observer transactions, stable versioned entity references and build migration. The experimental trusted native snapshots must never be accepted as arbitrary untrusted binary saves.
