## Independently demonstrable defect and strictly diagnostic repair

The frozen original human labels showed repeated first-noun-head errors: `trash can` reduced to `trash`, `can` or `trashcan`; `toilet paper` reduced to `paper`; `computer tower` reduced to `tower`. These are **real literal source noun chunks** that the pretrained parser can truncate. This specific defect was independently demonstrable from original free-form source text and the existing frozen predictions, not hypothetical model tuning.

Implemented a separate research-only **posthoc source-driven literal compound correction**, restricted to `trash can` and ordinary spelled variants, `toilet paper`, and `computer tower`. It is only applied when the original frozen model's noun head was a known truncated component, and the full literal compound occurs near the selected original source offsets. It never changes a production parser, live GameCore, weights, model service or previous frozen predictions.

Executed on the original fully labeled 480-case external Nr3D evaluation: **27 corrected cases, zero reversals** in the inspected sample. Correct strict categories increased from **309/480 (64.375%)** to **336/480 (70.0%)**, a posthoc apparent improvement of **+5.625 percentage points**. Counts by literal-source rule: `trash_can` **15**, `toilet_paper` **8**, `computer_tower` **4**. The experiment preserved all original source hashes and compared predictions with the same independently human-labeled reference target categories. The resulting input/output error differences were saved as separate private raw receipts. **Critical scientific limitation:** this is an *in-sample diagnostic*, rules chosen after seeing gold. It must **not** change the preregistered FAIL outcome, masquerade as heldout proof, or be treated as independent performance. It still misses the 75% threshold and cannot identify unique ScanNet 3D instance IDs, real geometry, supports or correct literary source semantics. Verify on NEW truly unseen source and independent author labels before deployment.
