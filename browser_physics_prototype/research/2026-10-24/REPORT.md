## Skill: measured provisional answer

`node browser_physics_prototype/research/2026-10-24/skill_hidden_study.mjs` runs real headless `GameCore` physics at 60 Hz, with an x-axis walk/brake skill and stop near x=-1.2. Four training episodes from distinct starting x positions generated **259 state/action pairs** through an engineered expert policy. A seven-neighbor non-neural clone uses current world x and x-velocity, retaining the core's actual forces, collisions and time. Heldout starts: -4,-3.9,-3.7,-3.6,-3.4,-3.3,-3.1,-2.9; no random frame split. Reference feedback expert: **7/8** completed, clone **7/8**, fixed 80-tick macro **0/8**. Some apparent false failures are velocity-tolerance edge cases (the success criterion checks |vx| < 0.5); preserve actual failures, do not relabel. Macro overshoots greatly because it lacks state feedback. Restore in the middle of eight heldout episodes per controller returned valid and matching player x in 8/8. Manual `Move(-1)` at tick 45 was accepted and `goal` was null in 8/8 for each controller; this is **only immediate one-step interrupt**, not a cancellation arbiter ensuring the policy does not reassert control on subsequent ticks. Gameplay ownership, human input and safe habit activation are not wired or tested.

This establishes feasibility of one elementary trained non-neural action controller in the existing 3D physics simulator, **not** reliable player-taught movement across varied 3D terrain, changing physics, contacts, injuries, equipment, interruptions, or saved controller state. No actual player demonstration was captured. No neural training, temporal policy, recency comparison, train-online performance or human playtest occurred. A robust tiny controller is still an open question; evaluate on real player trajectories and adversarial states with episode-separated splits, precise interruption semantics and termination guards.

## Hidden periodic/causal rules: measured provisional answer

The currently implemented rule is `resonanceAt(t) = 0.63 sin(2πt/8.2+0.18) + 0.37 sin(2πt/3.1+0.95)` and opens a gate when value >= 0.66. At 0.25-second samples over 60 seconds, 33/240 windows qualify (13.75%). Comparison fixtures: simple period 8s 72/240 (30%); another two-frequency sum 32/240 (13.33%); constant 0.7 240/240 (100%, trivially always-on). This opportunity-window statistic is **not** evidence of player learnability. Existing `tests/hidden-and-character.mjs` passed: both success/failure opportunities, a measurement, eventual true gate lift, physical transition and save/restore of gate state. It probes simulation mechanics, not discovery by a human.

Candidate fair mechanics: stable bounded single-frequency cycles for discoverable timing, composites with visibly available partial instrumentation for advanced inference, and observable causal graphs with reversible interventions and feedback. Unchanging always-on thresholds are trivial; excessively narrow multi-frequency windows with no clocks or feedback risk appearing arbitrary. Disclose through timestamped measurements, trends, persistent journals, controllable switches, distinguishable cause/effect, partial clues and timing aids. Provide predictable preview, bounded failure penalty, and player-authorized scheduling for very slow cycles. Fast rhythm must have perceivable anticipation cues and calibrated input tolerance; slow timing must allow simulation-time observation or automation instead of imposing real-life attendance. These are **testable design hypotheses**, not demonstrated engagement or balanced player difficulty. Add a preregistered blinded identification study comparing rule families by human inference accuracy, time to infer, incorrect models, and retained knowledge; separately evaluate fast/slow opportunity exposure and input latency.
