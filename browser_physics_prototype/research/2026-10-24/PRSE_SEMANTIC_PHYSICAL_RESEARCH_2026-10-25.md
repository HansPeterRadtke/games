## Priority and bottom-line outcome

The owner's defining PRSE research task is the persistent **literary/semantic world → validated three-dimensional entities → authoritative live rigid-body physics and observable events → truthful updated semantic/literary facts → economical off-screen causal story → consistent rematerialization**. This is more important than the optional player-trained skills previously studied. Read original recorded voice texts (see source ledger below). A model can propose a scene or prose; it does **not** get to override measured geometry, object identity, physical outcomes or what the player could have perceived.

This session produced two independent bounded evaluations: externally annotated **real-world language prompts** from SceneEval-500, and actual full XYZ rigid-body contact, snapshots and off-screen semantic state in the **existing PRSE GameCore Rapier world**, through a research-only sidecar. Meaningful negative results include low count/exact text parsing, missed support/wall/color relations, original locally recorded LLM numeric errors, a misleading rotational description found and corrected, and an unreconciled **global scene/physics clock discrepancy**. Neither this sidecar nor the scene parser is in the live PRSE server. There were **no new shared model calls**: active production Thor servers had occupied inference capacity, and Nitro CPUs were concurrently busy. Do not claim full general natural-language world generation or actual live game implementation from these results.

## Independent research: what methods are realistic?

**Holodeck**, Yang et al., CVPR 2024, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yang_Holodeck_Language_Guided_Generation_of_3D_Embodied_AI_Environments_CVPR_2024_paper.html , uses an LLM to propose spatial relational constraints and optimizes object layout. It supplies human-evaluated language-guided scene assembly precedent, not reliable history/causal continuity.

**PAT3D**, Lin et al., ICLR 2026, https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/f35d2be7bc4da374557da1f5ef06eeeb-Abstract-Conference.html , combines generated scene trees, rigid-body physics and physics-in-loop optimization to reduce penetrations and improve stability; the released benchmark is small (the published comparison includes eighteen prompts), and it does not establish that general offscreen story causality persists across gaming sessions. It supports a hybrid proposal+solver method, not asking an LLM to make contact calculations.

**SceneEval**, Tam et al., WACV 2026, https://doi.org/10.1109/WACV61042.2026.00710 and https://github.com/3dlg-hcvc/SceneEval , evaluates explicit object counts, attributes, spatial relations and implicit support/collision/navigation. The official v250610 release has one hundred manually authored prompts followed by four hundred semi-automatically extended/annotated examples. This is **not** evidence that every prompt can already be accurately materialized.

**Evolutive Scene Graph**, Fang et al., September 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15392 , describes dynamic timeline-constrained scene compilation, with ten benchmark scenes. The published demonstration is not equivalent to indefinite coherent offscreen literary gameplay.

**Scenethesis**, NVIDIA research, ICLR 2026, https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dir/scenethesis/ , separates language planning, perceptual asset/layout proposals and physical optimization, reinforcing that direct poses guessed by a model often need correction. These papers inform design. They are **not** new model calls or comparative scores measured on Nitro.

The sensible candidate architecture is therefore a verified textual/fact source and event ledger, model-based entity/relation proposals with provenance, deterministic schema/unit/spatial constraints and simulation-in-the-loop placement, real physics-step event extraction and visibility, a source-referenced semantic delta, model-generated literary *proposals* checked against current truth, and one global durable clock with explicit offscreen process scheduling. Cached scene IDs, rotations and damage persist. Raw old literary prose remains historical provenance, not unquestioned current spatial truth.

## External prompts — parsing, not geometry

The official **SceneEval-500_v250610.zip** was read only and preserved privately outside Git under the Nitro game research data area. ZIP SHA-256: 2948037f497c81cb2ce8241010c8d93250248217ca9961aa33e59092196b3676. The source-backed six selected rows, including *unaltered original descriptions and annotated counts/relationships/attributes*, were copied to sceneeval_six_source_annotations.json. The full downloaded benchmark remains external/private, not vendored.

sceneeval_baseline.py runs a simple finite-vocabulary quantity parser on the raw text, and compares against independent annotation counts; there are no generated 3D results in this phase. Metrics:

| Descriptions | Legacy four-role shelter converter | New expanded regex parser object-count F1 | Exact entire count-set rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First 100 human-curated | Cannot accept general prompts | 0.544 | 5% |
| Remaining 400 semi-automatically extended | Cannot accept general prompts | 0.346 | 0.25% |
| All 500 | Cannot accept general prompts | 0.378 | 1.2% |

This is an exploratory author-selected vocabulary and a **negative result**, not a holdout hyperparameter-selected model benchmark. The parser scores equality (`eq`) count annotations; inequality/at-least (`ge`) counts are not scored by this parser, and the presence of a negative phrase such as “no coffee table” is not correctly interpreted. Numeric equality scores are therefore not an exhaustive semantic benchmark. Example source row seven says there is *no coffee table*; the regex parser erroneously predicts one. It also confuses aliases (television/TV) and often omits categorical details or relationships. The legacy converter's zero coverage is a *scope comparison*, **not** evidence that the learned model is inferior on matched data: it was designed to accept only four exact shelter roles.

A separate automatic **text → lexical counts → sized entities → real Rapier scene → snapshot and re-entry** path was tested on thirty-four sampled human-authored SceneEval prompts in prse_auto_external_text_to_physics.mjs. Thirty-three produced nonempty physically instantiated scenes and thirty-three survived serialization/rematerialization; one supplied no recognized objects. This is **pipeline viability only**: default size catalog, grid placement, no interpretation of attributes, wall constraints, support, room topology or semantic relationship validity. Relation and attribute success for this automatic baseline is **unverified/zero tested**. Its physical validation rate must never be mistaken for faithful text-to-world conversion.

## Full three-dimensional physics and semantic persistence feasibility in existing GameCore

prse_semantic_roundtrip.mjs is a **research-only** general-object sidecar within the real GameCore world. It does not create an unrelated second physics solver. The sidecar accepts bounded source-linked generic object types and three-dimensional sizes/positions, body type, material, relations and state, inserts **real Rapier rigid bodies** in the same existing GameCore physics world with all three object translation and rotation axes enabled, saves their actual positions/velocities/rotations, receives explicit physical impulses, and records displaced/rotated/tipped/collision event types. It also provides a scene-specific semantic event ledger, generated *current factual description*, a short deterministic changed-state narration with repeat suppression, selective materialization, and bounded deterministic offscreen event rules. A custom **wrapper snapshot**, not native GameCore save alone, carries the sidecar scene.

The external examples in prse_semantic_physics_study.mjs are **six manually mapped scenes** derived from *exact* SceneEval prompts, not automatically generated from those prompts. All six mapped scenes retained their source prose and stable entity IDs across physics and wrapper save/restore. The manually supplied source counts cover twenty-one annotated quantity constraints and satisfy them by **construction**; this is not an object-recognition success rate. A separate sceneeval_relation_audit.py scored the other twenty-five annotated attribute/relation requirements: **seven** coarse spatial/support checks passed, **zero** measurable failures in this hand-authored tiny subset, and **eighteen remain UNVERIFIABLE** without actual walls, door/camera orientation, color and other assets. These omissions prohibit claiming complete meaning preservation.

A more physical original-recording-inspired workbench scene used two ceramic pots and a rolling sphere. The Rapier contact solver produced actual contacts of the ball with the bench and one pot, with logged relative speeds of about 1.12 and 0.90 meters per second. A force produced displacement; the event projection narrated the verified contacts without repeatedly narrating them after the observer cursor. Original first-pass data incorrectly labeled a sphere rotating as “tipped over.” The second pass fixed the category distinction, retained the negative first result, and reported **rotation**, not tipping. The initial hand-authored ball position also extended outside its support and was rejected; this was corrected in the later test plan without erasing the failure.

The offscreen test advanced a paper note from intact to scorched to ash via a **specific explicit ignition action and an authored time-dependent semantic rule**. It is *not* actual thermochemical or collision-triggered fire. The note's historical ID remained a destroyed-object tombstone, while the desk physically rematerialized; the current factual story no longer claimed that a readable note remained. Without a trigger, a separate no-cause test left the note intact even after sixty seconds, avoiding hallucinated spontaneous fire.

A separate adversarial real-3D motion test applied a physically sufficient nonzero x/y/z impulse to a wooden crate, moving it roughly **2.87 meters along z**, a direction unavailable to the original constrained player. After saving, restoring and stepping both original and restored trajectories another seventy ticks, the difference was approximately **seven micrometers**. This establishes actual simulation and state continuity for *that* object and interval, not universal replay determinism. A weaker impulse moved the crate only three centimeters because floor friction halted it, a preserved negative result and not a fake “third-axis motion” win.

Geometry validation rejected four known-invalid plans: overlapping objects, a floating object explicitly claiming floor support, a duplicated identity, and a missing support. This test does not cover every spatial failure mode or offscreen contradiction. The current descriptive source is immutable history while a separate current factual prose field is derived deterministically; **literary fluency and retention of richer narrative context are still insufficient** and need grounded model rewriting tests.

For synthetic scale only, physics was stepped fifteen ticks with approximately ten, thirty, one hundred and three hundred research rigid bodies. The results recorded actual object counts, wall time and JSON snapshot bytes in prse_semantic_physics_results_v4.json. On this heavily shared host, example times ranged from tens of milliseconds to approximately half a second per fifteen-tick batch, and the three-hundred-object wrapper JSON was about two hundred and thirty kilobytes. **These are not stable steady-state frame-time, end-to-end player or model latency benchmarks**; they use simple objects and exclude rendering/model calls.

## Actual remaining problems, including a measured integration defect

The biggest open question remains not a parser score but continuous authoritative world synchronization. In the offscreen feasibility adapter, the narrative clock advanced **about sixty seconds** while native GameCore's clock remained at its prior value. This is an observed unresolved **time reconciliation defect**: moving this adapter into the live game without a unified event/scheduler timestamp would allow contradictory histories. Fixing this requires reconciling all other world timers and queued physical processes rather than silently changing a single counter.

Additional generalization gaps: scene geometry lacks room-wall/door navigation, visual appearance, compound object supports, true fracture and large object damage; event extraction covers only limited materialized physical contacts and changes (contact monitoring intentionally skipped beyond forty bodies); descriptions derived from facts have low literary quality; no reliable event-to-rich-prose model was evaluated on general independent scenes; no full-state restoration of every Rapier manifold and periodic process is guaranteed; and the sidecar's wrapper snapshots/objects are **not integrated into the live GameCore semantic streaming loop**. The original user's game player still normally moves on a restricted plane; research objects can move in all three dimensions. This distinction must not be hidden.

Prior canonical main dirty changes appear to include a **separate currently uncommitted candle physics/contact/ignition implementation**, which this isolated branch did not modify. Do not merge overwriting that concurrent development. No PRSE live sessions, commercial renders, model servers, or production state were changed.
