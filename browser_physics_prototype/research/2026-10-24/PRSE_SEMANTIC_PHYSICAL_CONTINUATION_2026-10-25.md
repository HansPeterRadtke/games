## Summary of this independent continuation

The previous research-only PRSE sidecar advanced its own literary clock by sixty seconds while the actual GameCore clock and scheduled processes did not advance. This was a concrete correctness failure, not a stylistic issue.

The sidecar now steps *visible* objects through GameCore.step(), which owns actual physics, semantic updates and world time, rather than invoking the Rapier solver behind GameCore's back. For *unloaded* scenes, the sidecar advances native GameCore time, SemanticWorld coarse quarter-second ticks, existing WorldConsequences scheduled effects and its own source-linked offscreen events, without stepping physical rigid bodies or pretending that any physics frame elapsed. The wrapper snapshot format is intentionally version two and rejects original inconsistent research snapshot format one and forged mismatched clocks; the research-only sidecar was never a deployed user-save format.

The new prse_clock_native_coarse_continuity.mjs study tested **the actual native PRSE seven-step shelter fire process in parallel with another source-linked offscreen paper event**, for a forty-six-second unloaded interval. The uninterrupted run and a save/reload-in-the-middle run agreed on the seven event types, original cause/outcome, scheduled timestamps and final native world states. The research scene, GameCore and its semantic accumulator agreed on time, the physics step counter did not advance while unloaded, and the sidecar resumed physical stepping on re-entry. The separate paper was reduced to ash at its original scheduled time, not retimestamped to the player's return time. Raw first positive outputs and later revised outputs are preserved separately.

### Why matching fire time does not mean all world simulation is correct

An additional counterfactual test, prse_clock_counterfactual.mjs, compared actual GameCore physics stepping for forty seconds against unloaded semantic-only advancement from independently initialized but equivalent causal contexts. Both produced the same seven official PRSE fire history events at the same times, with the same note, bench and shelter conditions. The native semantic clocks also agreed. The unloaded path did NOT step the ragdoll or player/physics world: native physics step counters differed by twenty-four hundred, and character hydration differed by about 0.16 percentage points. Whole GameCore state is thus explicitly **not equivalent**. The one shared-machine CPU measurement was about 3.2 seconds for physical stepping versus 0.042 seconds for semantic-only progression, but heavy concurrently running jobs and differing work make this a **single bounded feasibility measurement**, not a stable player frame-time or generic performance promise. The earlier test briefly failed from an **overstrict byte-for-byte full-ragdoll snapshot oracle**: independently initialized Rapier ragdolls had minute initial float/contact differences despite equivalent relevant cause/timing. We narrowed the oracle to source, scheduled events and task-relevant conditions, and retained the initial drift in the result JSON.

Coarse advancement refuses a still-active native GameCore player goal; it does not silently skip that goal's execution. It is a **regional research algorithm**, not a global fast-forward mechanism. Other native gameplay costs such as fatigue, hydration, moving active NPCs and player timers require explicit scheduling/ownership rules. No single timestamp patch should be mistaken for completion of the full globally persistent world.

## Actual materialized physical ash after offscreen destruction

The prior research adapter retained the original destroyed note ID but removed its physics body while simultaneously writing prose that ash remained. This gap could not establish physical rematerialization. The sidecar now keeps the note as a stable semantic tombstone, creates a **distinct real Rapier fixed ash-patch rigid body** when a paper item is destroyed, and attaches a durable originId/source reference to its original note. The generated remnant uses a deliberately coarse thin collider, and rests on its surviving support or the floor. It is not a simulation of paper combustion, ash particles, heat or fragmentation. The original source literary prose stays immutable; internal current prose derives from the saved entity facts. A deduplicated observer projection says that ash remains once rather than describing the destroyed note and its remnant as two unrelated live objects.

The prse_clock_native_coarse_continuity.mjs and prse_observer_relation_continuity.mjs follow-up checks pass across saving/re-entry. A long original ID is deterministically shortened with a hash while retaining explicit lineage. The negative capability checks in prse_ash_lineage_capacity_guard.mjs confirm forged origin IDs are rejected, a region at the upper entity limit refuses an unrepresentable new remnant before time changes, and an active player goal cannot be skipped. **At the full-capacity limit, an already ignited note can remain scorched with a pending unsatisfied event**; a production implementation must define capacity eviction, transactional rollback or a semantic-only remnant policy. This is a retained edge-case blocker, not silent success.

## Observability and causal relation corrections

The previous sidecar narrative methods defaulted to all scene IDs. That meant any caller could accidentally narrate an offscreen fire or a hidden damage event without the character perceiving it. The adapter now defaults to the **actual GameCore player position** and conservative distance-based nearby IDs. The observer-scoped event and current-state projection leaves remote events hidden, reveals the verified aftermath upon return, and suppresses already displayed event sequences. The old original source prose is never accepted as authoritative after changed state. A deliberately forged stale prose assertion was recomputed from the current saved facts. This boundary is still **distance only**: it has no verified occlusion, field-of-view or sensory evidence, and is not a complete user-perception model.

A separate real Rapier impulse moved a ceramic pot off its wooden support. The canonical structured semantic record removed the stale support relation, emitted a cause-linked lost-support event, and kept the changed relationship through wrapper save/reload. The adapter does **not** prove arbitrary relations such as wall-mounting, nesting, behind, inside, rotated-support contact or all human scene-language constraints.

The first observer-filtered rerun produced zero narrated physical events because the research observer was far from the scene; that was appropriate nonleakage, but would be a false positive if misread as fidelity. We corrected the test oracle to place a verified observer near the physical event and required real impact/change messages to appear once. The final prse_native_clock_observer_scoped_physics_regression.json records actual solver contacts with workbench and ceramic pot, a non-repeating evidence-backed description, physically created ash remnant, no premature offscreen story leak, six externally written manually mapped rooms retaining IDs/source facts, four invalid plans rejected, and bounded ten/thirty/one-hundred/three-hundred-body timing. Earlier first-pass outputs are preserved as diagnostics.
