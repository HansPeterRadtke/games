## Referential hard cases and original source scene validity

In original human scene 28, author text assigns one bed to a study room and another bed to a bathroom. The compiler nevertheless has **one** bed in a single generic provisional world. Its blanket-on-bed native collider contact can be real, and the human source can assert blanket on a study-room bed, while the generated single bed still cannot be aligned to the correct *persistent* authored bed or room. The case explicitly demonstrates conflation of two distinct referents; it is **not** an exact-instance success. Multi-room errors remain widespread in the human benchmark.

The original independent reference JSONs contain 6844 object rows, with **2938** `id` strings encoding child-parent hierarchy using `|`, but **2950** rows without `object_name`. Therefore rows without `object_name` are **not all unlabelled objects**; their actual reference identity can often be read from `id`. Moreover, `object_name` sometimes designates a group instead of the underlying physical instance: 50 rows have an `id` prefix different from `object_name`, 51 repeated (room, `object_name`) rows exist, and six repeated `id` rows exist. Some groups contain several separately instantiated objects. Previous named-object category matching alone was **not** authoritative unique-instance gold. The reference itself is not guaranteed to physically fulfill every authored clause (for example, some source-intended blanket or computer support may be missing or represented by a different asset category). Never silently promote these reference labels into exact physical or literary truth.
